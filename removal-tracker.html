Step-by-step: the two highest-impact removals

Start here. Spokeo and Whitepages are the most-trafficked and feed the widest exposure.

Spokeo — walkthrough (~5 min, Easy)
  1. Go to spokeo.com and search your name, filter to your state. Find the listing showing your city/address and rough age. Open it.
  2. Copy the full profile URL from the address bar (looks like spokeo.com/First-Last/...).
  3. Go to spokeo.com/optout.
  4. Paste the profile URL, enter an email you control, complete the checkbox, submit.
  5. Open the confirmation email from Spokeo and click the link. Removal isn't final until you click that link. Takes effect within a day or two.
Whitepages — walkthrough (~8 min, Hard: phone verification)
  1. Go to whitepages.com, search your name + city, state, and open your listing. Copy the listing URL.
  2. Go to whitepages.com/suppression-requests.
  3. Paste your listing URL and follow the prompts to identify the record.
  4. Give a reason (e.g. "privacy") and provide a phone number — Whitepages calls or texts a verification code. Enter it to confirm.
  5. Submitted requests process within ~24 hrs. This one is the most annoying but worth doing early.
The general pattern (applies to almost every broker below)
  1. Find your specific listing on the site (name + city + rough age to confirm it's you, not a namesake).
  2. Copy the URL of that listing.
  3. Open the site's opt-out / removal page (links in the tables above).
  4. Paste the URL, provide an email (use one address consistently so you can track confirmations).
  5. Click the confirmation email if one is sent — many removals silently fail if you skip this.
  6. Note the date in the "recheck" box. Brokers re-scrape public records, so listings often reappear in 3–12 months.

Reduce re-population at the source

The people-search sites above pull from a smaller layer of data aggregators (Tier 4). Opting out there slows how fast your info comes back everywhere else. Also worth doing once:
  • DMAchoice — reduces direct-mail marketing data.
  • LexisNexis opt-out — major background-data source.
  • Utah county property & voter records are public and can't be removed — that's the original source most brokers scrape. Removals are therefore recurring, not permanent.
